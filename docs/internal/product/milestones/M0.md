# M0 — Workspace, contracts, and quality gates

**Kind:** crate delivery ·
**Depends on:** M-1 ·
**Serves:** FR-44, FR-50, NFR-2, NFR-40, NFR-42, NFR-50, NFR-51, NFR-52, NFR-53, NFR-55, NFR-56

⚠️ Kept on one line even though it is over the file's usual width:
`check-requirements-trace.sh` reads the `**Serves:**` line, not the paragraph,
so a wrapped continuation is silently dropped and reads as drift against the
roadmap.

⚠️ **Six of those were added when M0 was decomposed** (`M0.0`), because the
tasks the plan itself lists serve them and a plan that under-names what it
serves reads as narrower than the work is. The error taxonomy carries FR-44's
redaction rules; `bin/oqueue` is FR-50's single binary; the workspace must check
on both Linux targets (NFR-40) and build with only cargo and a C compiler
(NFR-42), which is what makes the allocator choice an ADR rather than a
preference; M0.1 corrects the two files that still say M-1's gates do not
exist, which is NFR-50's every-commit-green claim resting on gates a reader is
told are absent; and the leaf-crate split's inlining ADR decides how much of NFR-2's
10 ms cached-read budget is spent on calls that could not be inlined across an
rlib boundary. ⚠️ **M0 decides NFR-2's exposure and measures nothing** — M14
measures. ⚠️ FR-31 and FR-41 are deliberately **not** here: M0's `ObjectStore`
and `KeyProvider` fakes serve NFR-51 — the seam exists and nothing outside it
does I/O — and the behaviour those requirements describe stays with M1, which
writes the S3 and GCS backends, and M8, which writes the KMS providers.

⚠️ **Including NFR-2 while excluding FR-31 and FR-41 is not inconsistent, and
the line between them is worth stating** because "M0 does not implement this"
is true of all three. The test is whether M0's artifact changes the
requirement's *outcome*. It does not for FR-31 and FR-41: M1 and M8 write the
S3 and GCS backends and the KMS providers, and nothing M0 commits makes that
work easier or harder — citing them here would make those milestones read as
refinements of a requirement already served. It does for NFR-2: an
un-inlinable crate boundary chosen in M0 is paid on every cached read for the
life of the project, and M14 only *measures* that outcome — it does not get to
revisit it. A decision that permanently spends a budget serves the requirement
that budget belongs to, even when the milestone making it takes no
measurement.

## Goal

After M0 there is a Rust workspace that builds on both Linux targets, eleven
crates arranged so the dependency DAG is **wide and shallow**, and every trait
seam in `oqueue-core` has a fake beside it. No broker behaviour exists yet —
what exists is the shape everything else is written into, and the gates that
keep that shape from eroding.

⚠️ **This is the milestone where two constants stop being underived.** NFR-55
(coverage) and NFR-56 (pre-commit budget) cannot be chosen without something to
measure, and M-1 deliberately left them blocked rather than guessed. Choosing
them here is not a formality — a guessed threshold is indistinguishable from a
measured one a month later.

## Depends on

**M-1**, entirely. Every task here is executed against the standards and gates
M-1 produced, and several M-1 gates (`check-layering.sh`, `check-sans-io.sh`,
`check-unsafe.sh`, `check-core-contract.sh`, `check-file-size.sh`) have never
run against Rust code — M0 is their first real exercise, and finding they were
written against an imagined codebase is an expected outcome, not a failure.

## Decisions required first

| Decision | Why now | Background |
|---|---|---|
| Async runtime and its place in the crate graph | Every crate that does async is affected, and doc 10 #32 wants a spike before committing — ⚠️ doc 10's resolved log said madsim swaps the runtime by `cfg` rather than changing crate structure, so this looked smaller than it otherwise would — **`M1.22` (2026-08-21) found that premise false** (no madsim shim exists for the `reqwest`/`hyper` stack), though "smaller" held anyway, for a different reason: see `standards/testing.md`'s "Deterministic simulation" section | doc 05 §3, doc 10 #32 |
| Allocator | Doc 18 §3.5 leans mimalloc with jemalloc feature-gated for heap profiling; snmalloc is unbenchmarked and its message-passing free design matches our allocation shape exactly | doc 18 §3.5, doc 10 #30 |
| The five-profile `Cargo.toml` | ⚠️ `lto = false` with `codegen-units = 1` performs **no LTO at all**, and `cargo bench` inherits `release` — both are silent | doc 18 §3.1–3.2 |

⚠️ **Crate boundaries are optimization barriers.** A non-generic `pub fn`
without `#[inline]` is not inlinable across an rlib boundary without LTO, and
doc 18 §0.4 calls this "ruinous" for a byte-level codec split across crates.
The architecture splits `oqueue-buf`, `oqueue-codec`, `oqueue-checksum` and
`oqueue-index` into four crates, so either every hot-path entry point crossing
those boundaries is `#[inline]` **or** the split needs an ADR. Decide it here,
not when M2 is slow.

## Completion condition

```bash
scripts/gates/m0-complete.sh
```

Asserts: the workspace builds on `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu` and
`aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu`; every `pub trait` in `oqueue-core` has a fake in
`oqueue-testkit`; `check-layering.sh`, `check-sans-io.sh` and `check-unsafe.sh`
pass; **both** NFR-55's coverage constant and NFR-56's pre-commit budget hold as
numbers in their scripts rather than environment variables; and each was
measured on this workspace rather than carried in.

⚠️ **"A fake in `oqueue-testkit`" contradicts this plan's own Goal section and
two standards.** The Goal above says "every trait seam in `oqueue-core` has a
fake beside it", which is what `contracts.md` rule 9 and `testing.md` rule 4
require — a fake beside its trait in `oqueue-core`, so a downstream crate is
testable without a testkit dependency — and `contracts.md` rule 11 calls a fake
found in `oqueue-testkit` a layering violation. The Goal is right and this
condition is wrong. Provisional task 10's "fake-per-trait convention enforced"
inherits the same error. Both sentences are left as written, for the same reason
as the paragraph below. ⚠️ `m0-complete.sh` asserts the standards' location, per
the backlog's M0.18 row.

⚠️ **"Builds on `aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu`" was written before anyone checked
whether it could.** It cannot in M0: there is no cross-linker, so `cargo build`
for aarch64 fails at link the moment `bin/oqueue` exists, and supplying one is
M13's work (`cargo-zigbuild`, the glibc floor). M0.12 and M0.18 therefore
require `cargo check` on aarch64 and `cargo build` on the host only. The
sentence above is left as written — this is a plan, and editing it to match
what M0 could actually do would erase the evidence that the constraint was
missed. ⚠️ Whoever writes `m0-complete.sh` must encode the backlog's criterion,
not this paragraph's original claim.

⚠️ **And the aarch64 wall is earlier than "no cross-linker" says.** Measured
when `M0.18` wrote the gate: `cargo check --workspace --target
aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu` fails on `bin/oqueue` *before rustc runs at all*,
because `libmimalloc-sys`' build script looks for `aarch64-linux-gnu-gcc` and
exits `ToolNotFound`. That is ADR-0007's recorded cost, not a linker question,
and it means even the `cargo check` half of the condition cannot cover the
composition root on a host without a cross C toolchain. `m0-complete.sh`
therefore narrows the non-host target's check when no such compiler is present,
says so in its output rather than silently narrowing, and checks the whole
workspace where one is.

⚠️ **Two clauses here were true of M0 and are false now; `M1.42` corrected
them rather than deleting the paragraph, because the reasoning above still
holds.** The exclusion was `--exclude oqueue` alone, and "everything under
`crates/` is pure Rust and is checked on both targets" was the sentence that
made this a complete argument for NFR-40. `M1.15` ended both: `ADR-0012` chose
`ring`, whose build script runs `cc` for the target, so `oqueue-store` needs
the cross toolchain exactly as `bin/oqueue` does. The gate now excludes both.
⚠️ **Whoever reconciles this gate against M0's completion condition must not
"restore" the single exclusion** — it fails, reproducibly, with
`cc-rs: failed to find tool "aarch64-linux-gnu-gcc"`. What NFR-40 gets from M0
is therefore narrower than this paragraph originally promised, and
`roadmap.md`'s deferral table carries the remainder into M13.

⚠️ **NFR-56 is named explicitly because it is the one most easily skipped.** M0
is its only possible source — the roadmap lists no other blocker — so a
completion condition that checks coverage and forgets the budget leaves
`check-budget.sh` holding a guessed number permanently.

## Risks and open questions

- ⚠️ **The eleven-crate split is asserted, not measured.** Doc 19 says DAG depth
  sets the build-time floor, and the split is designed for that — but no oqueue
  build has ever been timed. If `oqueue-core` approaches ~40 traits it becomes a
  rebuild bottleneck for the whole workspace; the pre-planned fix is splitting
  `core-types` from `core-traits` (doc 10 #34).
- **`check-*` gates written before any Rust existed will be wrong in places.**
  Expect to fix them here. A gate that passes because it matches nothing is the
  failure mode to watch for — M-1.15's negative tests are what catch it.
- **Neither constant has a defensible starting guess.** Two minutes is a common
  pre-commit figure in general practice; ⚠️ **the corpus states no coverage
  threshold at all** — doc 19 argues only that coverage is weaker than mutation
  testing. Do not go looking for a band it does not contain. Both numbers must
  come from this workspace. ⚠️ Recording a number without measuring it here would make
  every later threshold argument rest on a fiction.
